Sea of Uncertainty

An operational naval wargame of command, information, tempo, and combat.

The player should still move the pieces and fire the weapons — but the quality of those moves and attacks should depend on command, information, timing, and friction.

1. What You Are Commanding

Each player commands a naval/joint operational force. The basic maneuver piece is the Formation, not the individual ship.

2. The Map

The prototype uses large operational hexes, initially around 50 nautical miles per hex. Scenarios may alter scale. The hex map preserves screening, flanking, chokepoints, patrol lines, threat envelopes, standoff distance, and the simple pleasure of deciding exactly where a formation should go.

3. Time

There are no conventional alternating turns. Each Formation has a Ready Time on an Operational Time Track. Actions consume time; when the formation acts, determine the action duration and place its Ready marker that many spaces ahead.

00  01  02  03  04  05  06  07  08  09  10  11  12
                    ▲ CSG acts
                              ▲ next Ready Time

Forces therefore do not all act together. Tempo becomes an emergent property of preparation, friction, cohesion, and command.

4. Missions

Every Formation maintains a standing Mission until changed.

ElementQuestion
TaskWhat are you doing?
ObjectiveWhere or against whom?
PostureHow aggressively?
TriggerWhen should the formation deviate from routine execution?

Typical Tasks: Move, Patrol, Screen, Search, Intercept, Strike, Escort, Defend, Shadow, Withdraw, Replenish, Support.

Example: TASK: Screen • OBJECTIVE: Amphibious Group Alpha • POSTURE: Balanced • TRIGGER: Intercept hostile surface force within two hexes.

5. Command Attention

Headquarters possess a limited number of Command Slots. Routine execution does not use a slot. Intervention does.

FLEET HQ — 4 SLOTS
[ PLAN STRIKE ]
[ DIRECT CSG  ]
[ MONITOR BRAVO ]
[ FREE        ]

A slot may be committed to changing a Mission, coordinating a major operation, pushing through friction, focused intelligence, restoring disrupted command, close supervision, or contingency planning.

6. Information

Players normally see contacts rather than enemy formations. A Contact tracks:

  • Location Certainty: Low / Medium / High
  • Identity Certainty: Unknown / General / Identified
  • Age: 0 / 1 / 2 / 3+

Information ages. Old information becomes less useful for interception and attack and can eventually become Lost.

9. Signatures

Formation signature states are QUIET / NORMAL / LOUD. Active radar, high-speed movement, and major weapons use may raise signature. EMCON can lower signature but usually reduces Search effectiveness.

10. Friction

Friction primarily changes time. Complex or demanding actions may require a Friction Check.

d6Effect
1+1 Delay
2–5No additional delay
6Exceptional execution: −1 Time

High Tempo, low Endurance, disruption, and poor cohesion worsen the risk envelope. Preparation, local command quality, and appropriate mission posture improve it.

Push Through Friction

The player may assign Command Attention to reduce a delay. Doing so generates Command Strain. Repeated strain temporarily reduces the HQ’s available Command Slots.

11. Entropy

Entropy is the game’s name for military potential that still exists physically but is temporarily unavailable for effective use. It is not a second damage track and should not behave like generic morale.

Entropy is generated by five visible sources:

SourceExamplesTypical Game Effect
FrictionHigh-tempo movement, difficult coordination, complex actionsDelay / later Ready Time
DisruptionEW, cyber, deception, command attackReduced command, delayed or degraded information
DestructionDamage and combat lossesLoss of physical capability plus disorder
ExhaustionExtended operations / low enduranceHigher friction, weaker reaction, slower recovery
Mission ChurnRepeated retasking or intrusive command interventionPlanning loss, reduced cohesion, additional delay

Entropy State

For tabletop play, summarize accumulated entropy as Low / Moderate / High / Critical. The state is a compact operational indicator, but the formation should retain markers or notes showing the main causes.

EntropyMeaningTypical Consequence
LowMost physical potential remains usableNo broad penalty
ModerateNoticeable inefficiencyComplex actions become riskier
HighLarge share of potential unavailableMore delay, weaker coordination and reaction
CriticalFormation is physically present but operationally near-paralyzedMajor offensive action normally requires recovery or direct command effort

Reducing Entropy

Players do not normally spend a generic resource to “remove entropy.” They reduce its causes by choosing actions such as recovery, replenishment, restoring communications, simplifying a Mission, slowing tempo, or ceasing repeated retasking.

Design intent: a formation may still possess formidable ships and weapons while being temporarily unable to employ them coherently. That gap between physical capability and usable capability is the core purpose of Entropy.

12. Operational Cohesion

Cohesion is the player-facing summary of how well a formation can presently translate physical capability into coordinated action. Use three states: Cohesive / Disrupted / Disorganized.

Cohesion is informed by Entropy rather than replacing it. A formation with rising entropy is more likely to become Disrupted or Disorganized; recovery and successful command action can restore cohesion by addressing the underlying causes.

13. Endurance

Formation endurance is Ready / Extended / Critical. High Tempo and major combat operations accelerate degradation. Extended formations suffer minor friction increases; Critical formations face reduced movement and greater disruption risk. Replenishment restores endurance.

14. Reaction

When affected by enemy activity, a formation may receive a Reaction Opportunity based on readiness, information, posture, time, and command state. Possible reactions include maneuver, CAP / air response, missile defense, counter-detection, submarine evasion, breaking contact, launching ready aircraft, or executing a contingency order.

15. Strike Planning

  1. Select a target based on current information.
  2. Select participating forces.
  3. Select weapon / capability commitment.
  4. Choose timing: launch now or synchronize.

16. Synchronization

Multiple formations may deliver effects in the same operational window. Synchronized effects are valuable because they reduce the defender’s ability to recover and react between engagements.

   EW  ─────┐

   AIR ─────┘

The cost is planning time, Command Attention, and greater exposure to friction. The target may move; information may age; one element may be delayed.

17. Combat

Combat should remain tactile without becoming a platform-by-platform engineering simulation.

  1. Targeting: determine quality of target information.
  2. Weapon Allocation: attacker chooses how much capability to commit.
  3. Outer Defense: CAP / long-range interception.
  4. Area Defense: SAM and escort defenses.
  5. Terminal Defense: point defense, decoys, maneuver.
  6. Impact: surviving attacks generate Damage Effects.

Weapon Commitment

Greater commitment improves penetration but depletes future offensive capacity. The player should frequently ask: overwhelm this target now, or preserve striking power for a more important contact later?

Bounded Resolution

The player’s choices establish an Outcome Band before the die is rolled. The die chooses the exact result inside that band.

18. Damage

Physical damage states are Light / Heavy / Crippled / Destroyed. Scenarios may add component damage such as impaired flight operations, radar damage, reduced missile capacity, or mobility damage.

19. Deception

Deception may create false contacts or reduce enemy certainty. Its value is measured by what it causes the opponent to do: move the wrong formation, launch the wrong aircraft, spend command attention, delay a plan, or expend weapons against a false problem.

20. Command Disruption

  • Degraded Command: one fewer Command Slot.
  • Delayed Information: reports arrive later.
  • Broken Link: specified formation cannot receive a new Mission until restored.

Existing Mission Orders continue unless their triggers dictate otherwise.

21. Mission Command

A disconnected formation acts according to its current Mission, its Trigger, and its local Command rating. Strong local command enables adaptation; tightly controlled forces may hesitate or continue literally.

23. Operational Cycle

1

Situation Update

Ask: What changed? What new reports arrived? Which contacts are becoming stale?

Resolve incoming information, contact aging, and any delayed reports.

2

Command

Ask: Do I need to change a Mission? Where is command attention most valuable? What can I safely delegate?

Assign Command Slots, issue orders, plan contingencies, and prepare synchronization.

3

Ready Formations

Ask: Continue the current Mission, or intervene?

Every formation whose Ready marker occupies the current Time may act.

4

Movement & Search

Ask: How fast? How visible? How aggressively do I search?

Choose movement mode, search mode, and signature posture. Move the pieces and resolve detection.

5

Engagement

Ask: Attack now, hold, or synchronize? How much do I commit? What do I preserve?

Resolve strikes, reaction, defenses, leakers, and damage.

6

Friction & Next Action

Ask: Do I accept the delay, push through it, or recover?

Resolve endurance, cohesion, friction, and the formation’s next Ready Time.

Current design intent: one coherent game in which information determines what you think you should do; command determines what you can change; movement creates position; position and information create attack opportunities; combat and operational strain generate entropy; entropy makes part of physical capability unavailable and is summarized by cohesion; friction changes time; and time changes information and synchronization.
