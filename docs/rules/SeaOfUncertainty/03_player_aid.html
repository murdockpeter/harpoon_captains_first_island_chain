10-Step Sequence of Play

1

Advance Time

Who is ready to act?

Move the Clock directly to the next Ready marker(s). If both sides are ready: Lower Entropy → Higher Command → 1d6 tie-break.

2

Update Information

What do I actually know?

Age unrefreshed Contacts. Age 3+ contacts degrade Location; Low Location + Age 3+ becomes Lost.

3

Select a Ready Formation

Does its current Mission still make sense?

If yes, continue. If no, changing it immediately may require Command Attention.

4

Choose One Action

Move • Search • Strike • Patrol / Screen • Support • Recover • Replenish • Hold

5

Choose the Risk / Reward Posture

How hard am I pushing?

  • Move: Cautious / Normal / High Tempo
  • Search: Passive / Active / Focused
  • Strike: Light / Standard / Heavy; now or synchronize
  • Recover: choose eligible Entropy source
6

Enemy Reacts

Defend • Evade • Counterattack • Hold

Only when the action creates a Reaction Opportunity.

7

Resolve Search / Combat

What band did my decisions create?

Use the appropriate table. Then roll 1d6 to resolve the remaining uncertainty.

8

Apply Consequences

Update Contact certainty, damage, Signature, Endurance, Friction, Disruption, Destruction, and Weapon Expenditure.

9

Check Entropy

What is making this Formation less usable?

Friction □    Disruption □    Destruction □

0 = Low • 1 = Moderate • 2 = High / Disrupted • 3 = Critical / Disorganized

10

Schedule Next Action

Accept the delay or Push Through?

Apply the Action's Time Cost and Friction. Optionally use Command Attention to Push Through. Move the Ready marker.

Command Attention

Prototype baseline: 3 Command Slots per side.

Use them for Focused Search, immediate Mission change, Synchronized Strike, Push Through, Restore Command, or forcing a Critical-Entropy Formation into a complex action.

Before I Commit

  1. What do I know — and how old is it?
  2. What position am I trying to create?
  3. What am I exposing to get there?
  4. Do I act now or wait for better information / synchronization?
  5. How much capability am I willing to spend?
  6. What will the opponent be allowed to do in reaction?
  7. What Entropy am I creating or accepting?
  8. When will this Formation be ready again?
The dice should never be the plan. Your decisions create the band; the die reflects the uncertainty left inside it.
