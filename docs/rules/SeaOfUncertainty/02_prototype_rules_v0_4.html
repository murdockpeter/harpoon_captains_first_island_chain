Sea of Uncertainty

A playable operational naval wargame of command, information, tempo, and combat.

Ready → Choose → Act → Resolve → Friction → Schedule Next Action

How to Use This Draft

This document is the current playtest ruleset. It intentionally compresses the larger design into the actions a player actually performs at the table. The underlying theory remains present through time, information, command attention, entropy, synchronization, and friction — but the player experiences those ideas through concrete choices.

Core principle: the player should still move formations, search, launch strikes, react, defend, and inflict damage. The command-and-information model changes how well, how soon, and under what uncertainty those physical actions occur.

1. Advance the Clock

Move the Operational Clock directly to the next Time space containing one or more Ready markers. Do not advance through empty Time spaces one by one.

If both sides have Ready Formations at the same Time, determine order as follows:

  1. Lower current Entropy acts first.
  2. If tied, higher Command rating acts first.
  3. If still tied, each player rolls 1d6.

Players then alternate Ready Formations until all formations scheduled for that Time have acted.

2. When a Formation Is Ready

A Ready Formation chooses ONE Operational Action:

ActionPurpose
MoveChange position.
SearchFind or improve a Contact.
StrikeAttack a known Contact.
Patrol / ScreenEstablish a reaction posture.
SupportAssist another Formation or operation.
RecoverReduce operational disorder / Entropy.
ReplenishRestore Endurance or expended capability.
HoldRemain in place with minimal exposure.
Player question: Does the Formation's current Mission still make sense? If yes, continue. If no, changing it may require Command Attention.

5. Contact Age

Whenever the clock advances, increase the Age of Contacts that have not been refreshed.

AgeStatus
0Current
1Usable
2Stale
3+Very Stale

At Age 3+, reduce Location Certainty by one level whenever another Time interval passes. At Low Location + Age 3+, remove the Contact as Lost.

Place a Patrol or Screen marker and choose one posture:

A Screening Formation may react to threats approaching the Formation it protects.

Base Time Cost: 1 Time if nothing triggers the posture.

7. Strike

To Strike, the target must be represented by a Contact within weapon range.

A. Choose Target

Attack what your information picture says is present. That picture may be wrong.

B. Choose Commitment

CommitmentAttack ModifierCost

C. Strike Now or Wait?

Attack immediately, or declare a Synchronized Strike.

D. Defender Reacts

The defender chooses an available Reaction before combat is resolved.

9. Synchronized Strikes

Two or more Formations may coordinate attacks for the same intended Strike Time.

  1. Assign one Command Attention slot.
  2. Choose the intended Strike Time.
  3. Participating Formations wait until that Time.
  4. Resolve all participating attacks before the defender fully recovers.
Attack in SequenceDefender Penalty
FirstNone
Second−1 Defense
Third and later−2 Defense

While waiting, Contacts continue to age, targets may move, participants may incur Friction, and the enemy may act first.

Good attack now — or potentially devastating attack later?

10. Friction

Each Formation has three Entropy source boxes:

  • F — Friction
  • D — Disruption
  • X — Destruction

Mark Friction when:

  • Using High Tempo movement
  • Attempting complex synchronization
  • Operating while Extended
  • Rapidly changing Missions
  • Scenario conditions dictate

Mark Disruption when:

  • Affected by EW / cyber
  • Badly deceived
  • Command links fail
  • Certain combat effects occur

Mark Destruction when:

  • Suffering Heavy or worse physical damage

11. Entropy

Count marked source boxes:

Marked BoxesEntropy
0Low
1Moderate
2High
3Critical

Source Effects

SourceEffect
Friction marked+1 Time after complex Actions
Disruption marked−1 Search and synchronization effectiveness
Destruction marked−1 offensive capability

Two boxes marked: Formation becomes Disrupted; Reaction options are restricted.

All three marked: Formation becomes Disorganized; it may normally only Move, Defend, Recover, or Withdraw. Command Attention may force another action.

Why this matters: Entropy is not generic morale. It tells you how much of a Formation's remaining physical capability is temporarily unavailable because the organization is struggling to use it effectively.

12. Recover

Instead of moving or attacking, choose Recover and remove ONE eligible Entropy source.

  • Friction: removable through pause and reorganization.
  • Disruption: removable if communications / EW conditions permit.
  • Destruction: normally cannot be removed by ordinary Recovery; requires repair, replenishment, or scenario-specific capability.

Base Time Cost: 2 Time.

Tradeoff: Recovery improves later performance, but every Time spent recovering is Time given to the enemy.

13. Command Attention

For the initial prototype, each side begins with 3 Command Slots.

A slot may be assigned to:

  • Focused Search
  • Immediate Mission change
  • Synchronized Strike
  • Push Through Friction
  • Restore Command
  • Force a Critical-Entropy Formation to attempt a complex Action

Once the assigned task resolves, the slot becomes available again.

Command Attention asks: What is headquarters paying attention to right now?

14. Push Through

If Friction would add +1 Time, assign one free Command Slot to ignore that delay.

Then mark Command Strain.

At 2 Command Strain, lose one Command Slot until HQ conducts Recovery.

Operational meaning: you can absolutely insist that an operation launch now — but you cannot keep doing that everywhere without exhausting the command system.

15. Reaction

When attacked or intercepted, choose one available Reaction:

ReactionEffectTradeoff
Defend+1 DefenseNo offensive benefit
Evade+1 Defense; move one hex away after combatMay abandon position / mission geometry
CounterattackIf still combat-capable, immediately make a Light attackNo Defense bonus
Hold FormationNo modifierPreserves exact position and Mission

16. Hold

A Formation may remain in place, reduce Signature to Quiet if conditions permit, and preserve Endurance.

Base Time Cost: 1 Time.

17. Replenish

If scenario logistics permit, spend the action to restore selected capability:

  • Endurance
  • Weapon Expenditure
  • Repairable capability

Base Time Cost: 3 Time.

Tradeoff: Replenishment restores staying power but temporarily removes the Formation from useful operational tempo.

Sequence of Play

1

Advance Time

Move the Clock to the next Ready Formation(s).

If both sides are ready: Lower Entropy → Higher Command → 1d6 tie-break.

2

Update Information

What do I actually know?

Increase Contact Age, degrade very stale positions, and remove Lost Contacts.

3

Select Ready Formation

Does its existing Mission still make sense?

If yes, continue. If no, use Command Attention when required.

4

Choose One Action

Move • Search • Patrol / Screen • Strike • Support • Recover • Replenish • Hold

5

Resolve the Action

What risk/reward posture am I choosing?

Move: Cautious / Normal / High Tempo. Search: Passive / Active / Focused. Strike: target, salvo, now or synchronize. Recover: choose the Entropy source to address.

6

Enemy Reacts

Defend • Evade • Counterattack • Hold

7

Resolve Search / Combat

Decisions establish the odds. The die resolves the remaining uncertainty.

8

Apply Consequences

Update Contact certainty, damage, Signature, Endurance, Friction, Disruption, Destruction, and Weapon Expenditure.

9

Determine Entropy

Friction □   Disruption □   Destruction □

0 = Low • 1 = Moderate • 2 = High / Disrupted • 3 = Critical / Disorganized

10

Schedule Next Action

Accept the delay, or Push Through?

Take the Action's base Time, apply Friction, optionally use Command Attention, and move the Formation's Ready marker.

Player Card: When My Formation Acts

  1. What do I know?
  2. Where do I need to be?
  3. How much risk will I accept getting there?
  4. Do I need better information?
  5. Do I shoot now or wait?
  6. How much do I commit?
  7. What will this expose or consume?
  8. Do I accept friction or spend command effort overcoming it?
